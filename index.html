the work

Six months, one brand, and everything I learned about storytelling.

All five of these projects are from my time as Social Media Specialist at Noka Perfume (Feb–Aug 2023) — an Indonesian fragrance brand built on emotional storytelling. My role was to create Instagram and TikTok content that didn't just sell perfume, but made people feel something. Here's how I did it.

campaign strategy & copywriting

Meeting the audience in their actual moment.

Situation: Noka Perfume needed to run sale campaigns for major Indonesian shopping events — 6.6, 8.8, and payday sale — without falling into the trap of looking like every other brand screaming discount codes. In a crowded market, a sale post that only says "PROMO DISKON!" gets scrolled past.

Task: My job was to lead the concept and copy for these campaigns. The brief wasn't just "announce the sale" — it was to make the sale feel like it was for the audience, not at them. I was responsible for Instagram feed copy, Stories, Reels scripts, and coordinating brand collaborations.

Action: At first, I was still following the old approach because we were in the process of figuring out which direction we wanted to take. Once I understood that effective content is about actually telling a story rather than simply communicating a promotion, I started shifting my approach.

Instead of leading with a discount, I began with a feeling and a relatable situation. For the 25 July payday sale, I created a carousel that opened with a series of fake notification screens — a salary alert, a Shopee promo, a loan reminder, and a message from a partner asking for a treat — before eventually revealing the Noka promotion at the bottom. The idea was to capture the exact chaotic, relatable moment people experience on payday and naturally position the promotion within that story.

For the 6.6 campaign, I also coordinated a giveaway collaboration with Ella Beauty Store, designing the mechanics to generate brand exposure while simultaneously encouraging organic follower growth.

Result: The Giveaway x Ella Beauty Store collaboration post pulled 1,250 likes and 739 comments — Noka's highest-performing collaboration content during my tenure. The payday campaign concept was recognized internally as one of the most on-brand sale executions we'd done.

multi-phase campaign strategy · scripting · production

We didn't launch a box. We launched a promise.

Situation: Noka Perfume had a recurring operational problem: fragrance bottles were arriving broken due to inadequate packaging. Negative reviews citing damaged shipments were climbing. The team developed a new protective perfume case — the Encasement of Hopes — to solve this. But the challenge wasn't the product. The challenge was the launch: how do you turn a packaging fix into something customers actually care about?

Task: I was tasked with leading the full campaign concept and writing all scripts. The goal was to frame the Encasement of Hopes not as a logistics upgrade, but as proof that Noka listens — a brand commitment to its customers, not a product announcement.

Action: I designed a four-phase campaign arc:

Phase 1 — Bridging: Acknowledge the customer's existing relationship with the brand and build emotional connection before saying anything about a product.
Phase 2 — Teaser: Drop subtle hints. Something is coming, but don't reveal what.
Phase 3 — Problem Narrative: Surface the complaints directly. Name the pain. Show that we heard it.
Phase 4 — Solution & Launch: Reveal the Encasement of Hopes as the answer — not a product, but a response.

I wrote the script across the four phases. I also participated in filming portions of the background story footage myself, and coordinated the campaign timeline with my supervisor and the business development team.

Result: The campaign performed progressively, exactly as designed:

· Bridging Reel: 194 likes, 51 comments
· Story Reel: 256 likes, 69 comments
· Launch Reel: 316 likes, 136 comments — Noka's top-performing launch content

The comments on the launch reel showed customers responding to the story, not just the product.

brand identity · content strategy · soft selling

Why I never just 'posted' — and what I did instead.

Situation: Most brand Instagram feeds are a mix of product photos, promotions, and generic lifestyle imagery. Noka Perfume had 4 feed posts per month — a deliberately small number. Every single one had to count, and had to represent the brand's identity, not just fill a calendar slot.

Task: My role was to build a storytelling-first content strategy that turned every post — sale posts, product posts, testimonial posts — into a narrative that audiences would stop scrolling for. The brief was essentially: make people feel close to this brand.

Action: I approached every feed post with the same question: what is the human truth behind this? If I was posting about a sale, I found the emotional moment of a payday. If I was posting about a product, I found the feeling the product gave someone. I built 10+ audience personas monthly using Notion to understand not just who our audience was, but what they were thinking about on the day they'd see our content. I used Kalodata monthly to track what formats were resonating on TikTok, then translated those insights into Instagram's longer-form storytelling format.

The philosophy was simple: soft sell through belonging. A person who feels like a brand understands them doesn't need to be convinced to buy. They already want to.

Result: Noka's feed maintained a consistent voice and aesthetic identity across 6 months. Audience engagement on story-driven posts consistently outperformed product-only posts. The storytelling approach became the foundation all other content categories were built on.

content writing · audience education · storytelling

Making the science of fragrance feel like a conversation.

Situation: Fragrance is a category full of technical language that most consumers don't understand — top notes, base notes, longevity, sillage. Educational content about perfume risks feeling like a lecture. The challenge was making it genuinely useful without losing Noka's warm, intimate brand voice.

Task: Create educational content that taught the audience real things about fragrance — myth vs. fact, how to wear it correctly, how to make it last — while keeping it conversational, relatable, and on-brand.

Action: Instead of starting with information, I started with a real customer experience. One recurring complaint I noticed: customers saying their perfume seemed to last shorter over time after the first few uses. I took that real DM as the opening of the content — named the exact feeling the customer described — and then walked through the actual science behind it in plain, friendly language. Every educational post started with a human moment, then delivered the insight. Myth-or-fact posts were framed as gentle provocations ("Do you do this?") rather than corrections.

Result: Educational posts with a real customer message as the opening hook consistently generated higher save rates and comment responses compared to purely informational posts. Readers engaged because they saw themselves in the opening — not because they wanted to learn.

community storytelling · emotional copywriting · UGC strategy

I never posted a testimonial. I published a story that happened to be one.

Situation: Five-star reviews exist on every brand's page. Audiences scroll past them. A screenshot of a marketplace rating tells you nothing about why the product mattered. At Noka, I had access to something most social media managers don't use: real, personal conversations in the DMs. Customers weren't just leaving ratings — they were telling me about their lives.

Task: Transform raw customer feedback and DM conversations into content that made audiences feel something — not just see a positive review. The goal was to build community by showing that Noka's customers were real people with real stories, not just satisfied buyers.

Action: I developed a practice of actively engaging customers in conversation — asking follow-up questions, letting them tell me more than they planned to. When a customer messaged saying that after a long, frustrating day at work, catching the scent of Noka on her sleeve made her mood lift, I didn't post a rating. I built a carousel around that moment: opened with a literary quote about fragrance and feeling, revealed the DM conversation, closed with an invitation: "How about yours?" — turning the post into a community prompt.

For another post, a customer named Zaven mentioned in a DM that Noka was what made her feel confident enough to present at her thesis defence without hesitation. I structured a full narrative around her experience — the anxiety of presenting in front of examiners, the weight of that day, the small detail that helped her feel grounded — and only at the end revealed it was a testimonial. Then I connected her story to the launch of the Encasement of Hopes, making the UGC the bridge to a product campaign.

Result:
· Mood-lifting fragrance post: 130+ likes, 7 comments including audience sharing their own experiences
· Zaven's confidence story: 315 likes, 147 comments — one of Noka's highest-performing feed posts ever
· Multiple comment threads showed audience members sharing their own personal fragrance memories unprompted
